The problem it solves

Pharmacies adopt clinical services to grow revenue with the same staff. Owners then ask one question: did it work?

Two stores can post the same booking numbers and have opposite problems. One is out of pharmacist time. The other has empty gaps. One ROI story fits neither.

The diagnostic gives each store a before number, and a fair way to compare after.

Three signals that make the case

The data sits in the scheduler

MedMe describes a pharmacy-specific scheduler with multiple calendars, multi-service booking and custom appointment lengths.

Vendor-stated. Confirm field-level detail.

The levers already exist

Pharmacies can mark eligibility, control who gets booking links and limit waiting lists. Targeted SMS and email are listed too.

Vendor-stated. Confirm what fires by rule.

ROI is already the pitch

MedMe positions the platform as growing pharmacy services revenue with the same staff. The diagnostic proves it store by store.

MedMe’s own positioning.

Success and failure

Analytics sets the thresholds before the data pull.

Success

Pilot stores beat comparison stores on completed visits per pharmacist hour or no-show rate, in the direction their store type predicts.

Failure

No difference. Or the rules cannot separate stores into types.

What could change this

Strongest counterargument: MedMe may already report most of this. Then the diagnostic only relabels existing numbers and the value shrinks to the targeting rules and the comparison method. If existing analytics already do both, stop.
